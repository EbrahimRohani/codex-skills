# 90-minute Working Backwards walkthrough

Source: `Working_Backwards_Walkthrough_Deck.pdf`.

Use this sequence for a presentation or working session. Keep the fictional case clearly labeled and invite participants to challenge the promise, evidence, boundary, and owner.

| Segment | Time | Facilitation objective |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Why this matters | 4 min | Show how ambiguity creates risky handoffs when promise, evidence, or decision right is unclear. |
| The method | 6 min | Introduce the loop: customer need -> future promise -> hard FAQs -> evidence ledger -> decision. Repeat when an FAQ or evidence changes the promise. |
| PR/FAQ anatomy | 6 min | Explain that the press release clarifies value and the FAQs expose feasibility, quality, economics, and learning. |
| Fictional case | 6 min | Set the customer, promise, and in/out boundary; do not let the example become assumed policy. |
| Step 1: customer and outcome | 7 min | Align on one customer, one struggle, and one observable outcome before mechanism discussion. |
| Step 2: future promise | 7 min | Check value, observable flow, honest scope, and fallback. |
| Step 3: hard FAQs | 7 min | Ask what must be true across failure, accessibility, feasibility, custody, fairness, and cost. |
| Step 4: uncertainty | 7 min | Mark evidence, assumptions, experiments, and decisions; show a worked ledger row. |
| Step 5: recommendation | 7 min | End with a bounded recommendation rather than a broad launch request. |
| Step 6: decision rights | 6 min | Assign one DRI and a ratifying sponsor for irreversible exposure or investment. |
| Worked conflict | 5 min | Resolve "live pilot now or shadow test first?" with evidence, owner, reversibility, and commitment. |
| Experiment plan | 4 min | Stage evidence before exposure: prototype, shadow test, operations simulation, capped pilot, review. |
| Q&A and challenge | 15 min | Ask participants to challenge the promise, evidence, boundary, or owner and identify which experiment or decision must change. |

## Presenter cues

- Mechanisms are not the experience. Return to the customer promise when architecture or process dominates.
- Targets, illustrative numbers, and composite customer voice are not evidence.
- Broad collaboration is welcome; accountability remains singular.
- Every live promise needs a fallback, guardrails, a stop rule, and a named stop authority.
